# Tessary voice and tone

## Our voice

Tessary sounds like an experienced engineering peer.

We speak directly, explain our reasoning, and take clear positions supported by evidence. We assume the reader is capable and technically informed, without assuming they already know Tessary’s terminology or approach.

Our voice is:

- Direct, not blunt
- Candid, not performatively provocative
- Confident, not overconfident
- Technical, not needlessly complicated
- Conversational, not casual for its own sake
- Opinionated, but grounded in reasoning and evidence

## Speak like engineers talking directly

Write as you would explain something to another experienced engineer. Use plain language, concrete examples, and the necessary technical detail.

Do not add formality to sound authoritative. Authority should come from understanding the problem and explaining it precisely.

## Take clear, reasoned positions

Say what we believe and explain why.

Avoid hiding behind vague qualifiers or presenting every question as an unresolved tradeoff. When reasonable people could disagree, acknowledge the uncertainty without weakening the position.

## Leave the reader in control

Tessary’s writing should help readers feel:

- Informed about what is happening
- Calm enough to assess the situation
- Confident about what to do next
- Curious to investigate further

We communicate problems without manufacturing anxiety. Seriousness should come from the facts, not dramatic language.

## Lead with what happened

When reporting an issue, begin with what happened. Then provide the evidence, likely explanation, significance, and next step as appropriate.

Do not make the reader work through background information before understanding the issue.

## Separate facts from inference

Clearly distinguish what Tessary observed from what it concluded.

Present evidence as fact. Present interpretations, suspected causes, and recommendations as such. Do not make a conclusion sound certain merely to make the product appear decisive.

## Adjust tone to the situation

Tessary’s voice remains consistent, but its tone should reflect the seriousness of the context.

Routine communication can carry more character. Serious issues call for greater restraint and precision. Use judgment rather than trying to assign a different personality to every surface.

Tessary can use humor when it feels natural and appropriate. Keep it sparse. Never force a joke, and never let humor reduce clarity, trust, or the perceived seriousness of the situation.
