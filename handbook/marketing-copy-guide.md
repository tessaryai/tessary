# Tessary marketing copy guide

This guide defines how Tessary writes marketing content across the website, announcements, social media, emails, comparisons, and customer stories. Follow the Tessary [brand foundations](brand-foundations.md), [voice and tone](voice-and-tone.md) guide, [writing style guide](writing-style-guide.md), and [product glossary](product-glossary.md) alongside it.

## Start with what Tessary is

Tell readers what Tessary is before expecting them to understand why it matters.

The primary description should identify Tessary as an open-source agent reliability platform.

Use:

> Tessary is an open-source agent reliability platform for engineering teams running AI agents in production.

Do not lead with an abstract promise, category invention, or unexplained problem when the reader does not yet know what Tessary is.

After identifying Tessary, explain the problem it addresses, how it works, and why its approach matters.

## Use a clear message order

A complete Tessary marketing message should generally answer:

1. What is Tessary?
2. What problem does it address?
3. What does it do?
4. How does it work?
5. What evidence supports the claim?
6. What should the reader do next?

Short formats may answer only the questions relevant to their purpose. Preserve the order of understanding even when the message is compressed.

## Write for engineering leaders

Write primarily for engineering leaders responsible for AI agents in production.

They need to understand:

- What problem Tessary identifies
- Why their existing approach may miss it
- How Tessary’s approach works
- What adopting Tessary requires
- What evidence supports its claims

Do not assume that an engineering leader wants executive-level abstraction. Lead simply, then provide enough technical depth to make the explanation credible.

Other audiences may require different context, but the underlying description and terminology should remain consistent.

## Lead with the product, not the category trend

Talk about what Tessary does instead of opening with broad claims about the future of artificial intelligence.

Use:

> Tessary monitors every production trace and investigates changes in agent behavior.

Avoid:

> Artificial intelligence is transforming every industry, but enterprises face unprecedented challenges.

The reader does not need a market overview before understanding the product.

## Explain the problem concretely

Describe the observable problem and its consequences.

Use:

> Agents can develop production issues without crashing or returning obvious errors. Teams may not know that something changed until customers report it.

Avoid:

> AI reliability is more important than ever.

Specific problems make the need for Tessary understandable without manufacturing urgency.

## Connect benefits to capabilities

Explain the product capability that produces each benefit.

Use:

> Tessary monitors every production trace, so low-frequency changes are less likely to disappear inside a sample.

Avoid:

> Tessary gives you complete peace of mind.

Do not present an outcome without explaining how Tessary contributes to it.

## Substantiate claims with mechanism and evidence

A strong claim should explain both why it is possible and what supports it.

Mechanism explains how Tessary produces the result:

- Tessary evaluates every production trace.
- Focused classifiers check traces against defined conditions.
- Triage determines whether a finding represents a real issue.

Evidence demonstrates that the mechanism works:

- Product behavior visible in a demonstration
- Measured results from a controlled evaluation
- Data from real usage
- A customer result with appropriate context
- A reproducible technical example

Use the strongest evidence available. Do not imply that a mechanism alone proves an outcome.

## Make claims proportional to the evidence

State exactly what the available evidence supports.

Use qualifiers when they communicate a real limitation:

- In this evaluation...
- For the traces analyzed...
- The evidence suggests...
- Tessary identified this as the likely cause...

Do not remove necessary qualifications to make a claim sound stronger.

Avoid absolute claims such as “never miss an issue,” “eliminate failures,” or “guarantee reliability” unless they can be proved under the stated conditions.

## Show before claiming

Prefer demonstrations, examples, product output, and concrete measurements over adjectives.

Use:

> Tessary identified that tool-call errors increased from 2.1% to 5.8% after a deployment.

Avoid:

> Tessary provides powerful, industry-leading detection.

Let the reader reach the favorable conclusion from the evidence.

## Compare approaches directly

Tessary can take clear positions about competing approaches.

A useful comparison should:

- Compare approaches that address the same task.
- Explain the relevant difference.
- Use current and verifiable information.
- Apply the same standard to every approach.
- State meaningful limitations, including Tessary’s.
- Focus on consequences for the reader.

Use:

> Sampled evaluations reduce the amount of traffic analyzed, but they can miss low-frequency issues. Tessary uses focused classifiers that are cheap enough to run on every trace.

Avoid:

> Traditional evaluation platforms are broken.

Do not weaken another approach to make Tessary appear stronger. Acknowledge when another approach is appropriate for a different goal.

Name a competitor when doing so helps the reader make a real decision. Do not name competitors merely to borrow attention or create conflict.

## Distinguish facts from positions

Use direct language when stating a product fact:

> Tessary is open source.

Use reasoned language when stating a position:

> We believe production reliability requires monitoring every trace because sampling can hide low-frequency changes.

Do not present an opinion as an established fact. Explain the reasoning behind positions Tessary takes.

## Headlines

A headline should communicate what Tessary is, what changed, or why the reader should care.

Prefer:

- Clear meaning over intrigue
- Specific outcomes over abstract ambition
- Product relevance over wordplay
- One central idea

Avoid headlines that depend on the supporting paragraph to become meaningful.

Character and humor are welcome when they sharpen the idea. They should not obscure the subject.

## Announcements

State what is being announced early.

A product announcement should usually cover:

1. What is launching
2. Who it is for
3. The problem it addresses
4. How it works
5. What is available now
6. What the reader can do next

Do not make readers work through company history or market commentary before learning what launched.

Distinguish what is available now from what is planned.

## Customer stories

Build customer stories around a specific problem, change, and result.

Include:

- The customer’s original situation
- What they needed to understand or improve
- How they used Tessary
- What changed
- The evidence supporting the result
- Relevant limitations or context

Use the customer’s words when available. Do not convert a customer’s experience into a broader claim than it supports.

Prefer concrete results over general praise.

## Social media

Make each post understandable without requiring prior knowledge of Tessary.

Lead with the announcement, observation, result, or position. Add context before linking elsewhere.

Social posts can carry more character than product copy, but should still sound like Tessary. Do not adopt a different personality for engagement.

Avoid empty hooks, manufactured controversy, and claims that become misleading when separated from the linked content.

## Calls to action

Choose the clearest next step for the reader.

Use specific actions:

- Self-host Tessary
- Read the documentation
- View the example
- Explore the repository
- Request a demo

Avoid vague calls to action when the destination can be named:

- Get started
- Learn more
- Discover more
- Unlock reliability

A page can have different calls to action when readers are at different stages, but one action should be clearly primary.

Do not ask for more commitment than the preceding content has earned.

## Open source

Include “open source” in Tessary’s primary description.

Explain what being open source means in the relevant context, such as the ability to inspect the code, self-host Tessary, contribute, or understand how the system works.

Do not use “open source” only as a credibility signal. Connect it to something the reader can verify or do.

## Character and humor

Marketing can show more character than product or documentation copy.

Keep humor natural, sparse, and appropriate to the seriousness of the subject. It should come from a real observation or point of view, not from adding jokes to otherwise generic copy.

When humor competes with clarity or credibility, remove it.

## Avoid generic AI marketing

Do not rely on language such as:

- Transform your AI operations
- Unlock the power of AI
- Revolutionize agent performance
- Build with confidence
- Enterprise-grade intelligence
- The future of agent reliability

Replace broad claims with a specific problem, capability, mechanism, or result.

## End with understanding

After reading Tessary marketing copy, someone should understand:

- What Tessary is
- Who it is for
- What problem it addresses
- How its approach differs
- Why they should believe the claim
- What they can do next

If the writing creates interest without creating understanding, it is incomplete.
